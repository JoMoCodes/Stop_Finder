# Lens: Typography, Signage & Number Legibility

Reviewers largely agree the course's type system *works* at the design-language level — bold amber keywords, high-contrast white door plates, unmistakable red blanks — but breaks down at the edges: on canvas-rendered numerals under oblique viewing angles, in dense multi-clause prompt paragraphs, and in a quiz panel that never exploits typography to teach the very digit-place structure the course is built around. The strongest, most concrete critique (Ingrid) traces the door-plate legibility problem straight to the source: `font = 'bold 170px sans-serif'`. No reviewer flagged font choice as a serious *aesthetic* problem — every complaint here is functional: can the number be read, at this size, at this angle, from this distance, by this eye.

## The banner: works as hierarchy, fails as pacing

Nearly every reviewer likes the prompt banner's core typographic move — dark translucent background, bold **amber** (`#ffd9a0`/`#ffd54a`) keywords picking the verbs and nouns out of a paragraph of body text. Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Bea, Ingrid, Cyrus, Nadia, Omar, and Luna all independently praise this as clear, scannable, high-contrast type hierarchy ("that's textbook hierarchy, and it's intuitive" — Cyrus; "I can read this while standing in a doorway yelling at someone" — Bea).

But consensus (7 reviewers: Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Leilani, Omar, Luna, Kai) is that the paragraphs themselves are too long and too dense for a banner meant to be read in seconds. Rosa clocks it at "30–40 seconds of reading" against a driver's "90 seconds to scan a building." Priya notes the Part 5 prompt runs ~130 words across 5 sentences with the conceptual crux ("floor 1 holds the lowest numbers and the highest") buried mid-paragraph. Luna's phrasing: "Each banner contains 2–3 ideas... perhaps a one-line action at the top, with expandable details below." Ingrid is the only one to give this a concrete typographic fix rather than just an editorial one: bump line-height to 1.5+ (currently tight/default), and on mobile raise font-size from 15px to 16-17px. On mobile this becomes a hard failure, not just a pacing issue — Marcus, Priya, Leilani, and Omar all separately observe the banner text wrapping mid-phrase or getting clipped at 390px ("pattern going... then pick its" — Marcus quoting the actual mobile screenshot's broken line wrap).

Priya and Omar both, independently, flag the same specific typographic glitch: Part 3's building-name banner rendering as a bare "Building #" placeholder in muted grey-on-grey. Both describe it the same way unprompted — it "looks like a typo or missing data" (Priya) / "looks like a bug, not a design choice" (Omar) — rather than reading as an intentional fill-in-the-blank prompt. Both suggest a more explicit placeholder glyph (brackets, underscore) would fix it.

## Door plates and canvas numerals: the central technical finding

Ingrid's review is the most load-bearing single document for this lens. Her core claim: every door plate and building sign renders text via `ctx.font = 'bold 170px sans-serif'` — a generic CSS fallback rather than a specified stack — which means the actual glyph shapes, weights, and baseline metrics differ across Chrome/Safari/Firefox and Windows/Mac/Android, so "a driver using this tool on an iPhone 14 ... will see numerals that look different than a driver on a Samsung Galaxy in the same light." She also notes the numerals have no kerning or optical adjustment (a narrow "1" beside a wide "0" or "8" creates visible imbalance at 170px bold), and recommends either a full system-font stack (`-apple-system, Segoe UI, Roboto, system-ui, sans-serif`) or a single loaded web font for consistency, plus `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`-style tightening.

Dev (frontend engineer) independently corroborates the rendering-quality half of this from the code: the 512×256 canvas texture (1024×256 for larger labels) produces visibly soft/aliased text at scale, especially on Part 5's 5-digit numbers, and recommends bumping resolution to 1024×512 (or 2048×512 for large labels) or moving to a bitmap font atlas. Cyrus, focused on real-world signage practice, makes the same resolution/anisotropy point from a different angle — recommending `w=1024,h=512` textures and higher anisotropy specifically for side-facing plates, which take the most oblique angle. That's three independent voices (Ingrid, Dev, Cyrus) converging on "the canvas text needs more resolution and a real font stack," which is as close to full consensus as this lens gets on a single fix.

Notably, Viktor's description of the door numerals as "large black serifs numbers" doesn't match the code (`bold ... sans-serif`) that Ingrid quotes directly — worth flagging as a discrepancy rather than a finding, since Viktor was working from screenshots, not source.

## Legibility at distance and oblique angle

This is the second major consensus cluster (Cyrus, Hiro, Dev, Rosa, Bea, Marcus — six reviewers, from wildly different vantage points). Cyrus, a professional wayfinding/signage reviewer, names it directly: "Part 5 is the turning point... the canvas text starts to pixelate under perspective; the angle is steep enough that small text gets hard... real navigation is oblique. You're not standing three metres directly in front of a door." He proposes real-signage techniques the course doesn't use: pre-skewing the texture for the mounting angle, scaling up side-facing numbers relative to front-facing ones (as real gable-end signage does), and duplicating a sign on each corner.

Hiro (geometer) makes a related but distinct point about Part 5's four-face "Walk Around" views: because the right wall's numbering runs in the opposite direction from the left wall (bilateral asymmetry in the `SQ_LAYOUT`), and because the four canonical camera positions are all orthogonal 90° views, "the right-wall doors (617–620) are foreshortened and hard to read" in the default isometric framing — readability and comprehension of the pattern fail together at the same viewing angle. Rosa and Bea both flag specifically that Part 5's dense multi-floor door clusters (8+ visible plates at once) read as visually cramped/jammed, with Bea worried this is worse on a 390×844 mobile viewport where a driver "can't answer the question" if they can't read "617." Marcus separately confirms from the mobile screenshots that Part 5's door numbers are "rendered so small I can barely see the unit numbers."

## Blanks and partial-number masking

Ingrid raises a pedagogically important gap: the briefing describes partial masks like "71_5," but the actual code (`label(g, '?', ...)`) only ever renders a plain "?" — never the partial-digit variant. She also critiques the underscore glyph itself as ambiguous at this scale (sits at the baseline, can read as a minus sign or vanish in bold numerals) and proposes alternatives (a thicker offset underscore, a block character, a small square placeholder). Sam (accessibility) raises an adjacent but distinct problem with the same UI element: the blank plate is distinguished from a filled one *only* by color (red vs. white), which fails WCAG 1.4.1 for color-blind users (~7% of men) — her fix is a secondary non-color cue (dashed border, icon, or small text label) layered on top of, not instead of, the red. Omar separately confirms the term "partial number" in the prompt copy is never defined or exampled for a non-native reader. Nadia's review describes blank plates as inverting to a *green* background rather than red/white — this conflicts with every other reviewer's (and the briefing's) description and should be treated as a likely misreading, not a fourth data point.

## Quiz option typography: the notation should teach

Ada (mathematician) is a strong single voice on a point Ingrid partially echoes: the multiple-choice options are rendered as plain, unlabeled, left-aligned numbers with no visual structure, even though the entire pedagogical point of the course is a positional/mixed-radix numbering system. Her examples are specific — Part 2's options 122/222/322/422 (only the floor digit varies), Part 3's 6103/7103/8103/1103 (only the building digit varies) — and her ask is concrete: tabular/monospaced figures (`font-variant: tabular-nums`) so digits align in columns, plus per-digit-place color coding (building/floor/door each get a distinct color) so "the learner sees at a glance which digits vary and which stay fixed." She frames this as "like teaching fractions without showing the denominator." Ingrid's recommendation to use tabular-nums for door plates generally lends independent, if secondary, support to the same underlying fix.

## Contrast and accessibility of the type system

Sam's WCAG-oriented pass is the most rigorous accounting of color/contrast risk in the palette: she flags the amber-on-dark prompt/quiz text (`#ffd9a0` on `rgba(20,24,30,.86)`) and the red blank-plate text (`#c0392b` on white) as needing verification against 4.5:1, and separately flags the green "Correct!" text (`#7ee0a0` on dark) as a potential problem for color-blind users layered on top of the color-only-cue issue above. Ingrid, working from the same hex values, judges the amber and cream choices as already meeting WCAG AA and calls the system "deliberate and works" — a direct point of disagreement between the two typography-adjacent reviewers worth surfacing rather than resolving. Both agree, however, that focus states and keyboard-visible affordances are entirely absent from the button/option type system (Sam's finding, unopposed).

## Top recommendations for this lens

1. **Replace `sans-serif` with a real font stack (or a loaded web font) in every canvas `ctx.font` declaration**, and raise canvas texture resolution (512×256 → 1024×512 or higher) with increased anisotropy. — Ingrid (primary), corroborated by Dev and Cyrus.
2. **Shorten and re-chunk the prompt banner copy**; keep the amber-keyword bolding but cap it near one idea per sentence, and raise mobile font-size/line-height so text stops wrapping mid-phrase. — Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Leilani, Omar, Luna, Kai (consensus); line-height/mobile-size specifics from Ingrid.
3. **Fix the "Building #" placeholder** to an explicit fill-in glyph (brackets/underscore) instead of a bare, grey-on-grey "#" that two reviewers independently mistook for a bug. — Priya and Omar.
4. **Address oblique/side-view legibility of door numbers**, especially Part 5's four-face walk-around: larger or pre-corrected side-facing plates, or a call-out of the asymmetric numbering (Hiro's finding) so the hardest-to-read view isn't also the most pedagogically confusing one. — Cyrus, Hiro, Dev, Rosa, Bea, Marcus.
5. **Implement the partial-mask rendering ("71_5") the briefing calls for**, rather than a plain "?", and redesign the underscore/placeholder glyph so it doesn't read as a minus sign at 170px bold. — Ingrid; term "partial number" also needs a plain-language example per Omar.
6. **Give quiz options tabular/monospaced digit alignment and per-digit-place color coding** so the multiple-choice panel visually teaches which digit position is varying. — Ada (strong single voice), tabular-nums recommendation independently echoed by Ingrid.
7. **Add a secondary (non-color) cue to blank/partial door plates** — a dashed border, icon, or short text label — so the red-vs-white distinction isn't the only signal. — Sam.
8. **Reconcile the two contrast assessments of the amber/red/green palette** with an actual contrast-ratio check (WebAIM or similar) rather than relying on either reviewer's visual estimate. — Synthesis, prompted by Sam vs. Ingrid disagreement.
9. **Add visible `:focus-visible` states to quiz-option and navigation buttons.** — Sam.

## Out of scope / parked

- Sound effects / audio cues for correct/wrong feedback (Kai, several others) — requires audio assets, outside this lens and the no-build constraint concerns raised by the briefing.
- Lighting/shadow/sky-gradient overhaul for 3D realism (June, Felix) — a rendering/art-direction topic, not typography.
- Keyboard navigation and screen-reader alt-text for the canvas scene (Sam) — real accessibility work but belongs to an interaction/accessibility lens, not type/signage, beyond the color-cue point already folded in above.
- Curriculum-level changes: retrieval practice before quizzes, reordering Part 3's building-naming step, replacing Part 5's Walk-Around buttons with pure drag (Tobias, Viktor) — pedagogy/curriculum, not visual design.
- Score/streak/progress-bar gamification (Kai, Viktor) — game design, not typography.
- Physical-realism details: staircase modeling, window glazing, ground texture (Felix, Bea) — 3D asset/art direction, not type.
