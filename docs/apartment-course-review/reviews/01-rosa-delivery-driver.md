# Rosa Delgado, Delivery Driver (18 years)

## The Big Picture

This course works. The 3D buildings are clean, the doors pop out, the quiz is quick. A rookie could actually learn apartment numbering here instead of driving in circles at 9 PM on a dark street. That's solid. But there are three things that would speed up both the learning and the real route:

---

## What Works Well

**The visual hierarchy is sharp.** Top banner tells you what to do; green doors and hatches stand out from the tan/red walls. No confusion about what's clickable. In a dark lobby at 10 PM, I need to see the pattern fast—this course teaches that.

**Part 1 nails it.** The yellow arrows show left-to-right flow on a simple 4-plex. New drivers *need* this. 101-104 on bottom, 103-104 on top. That's the real world, and the arrows make it stick. I've walked into hundreds of buildings that look exactly like this screenshot.

**The complexity ramps right.** Parts 1→2→3→4→5 don't jump too hard. By Part 3, learners are reading 4-digit unit numbers and the "Building 1" banner anchors it. Part 4 flips to front+back (yeah, we deal with that all the time—doors on both sides). Part 5 is the hardest, which is correct: a 20-unit building counting 601–620 all the way around with no floor digit. The "Walk around" buttons are smart here.

**Buttons are easy to find and hit.** Green pill buttons at the bottom. "Jump to part" at the top left. Right-side quiz panel. Everything is where my hand expects it, no hunting around.

---

## What Drags Down the Stops Per Hour

**The instruction text is too long.** 

Look at Part 1: *"This is a common number layout you can find at most apartment buildings. The units count up in order — the yellow arrows show the direction the pattern flows..."* That's good context, but I'm reading a full paragraph. A rookie in training might, but on the actual route? I'd skim it and forget. Consider breaking key words into bullet points or a shorter pitch. Part 4's message starts with "This last course is the toughest"—nice heads-up, but the paragraph below runs long. Real world: I have 90 seconds to scan a building. This text is closer to 30–40 seconds of reading.

**The 3D camera needs guardrails.**

You can drag to rotate the building freely. Good for learning, but in the quiz stages (Parts 1–4), the building sometimes rotates so a door number is hard to read. In Part 1, stage 2 (01-part1-quiz-quizopen.png), if I drag the building around a few times, 110 or 112 might face away from me. A rookie might miss a number by accident. **Suggestion:** lock the front face in quiz mode, or snap back to the original angle after 2 seconds of no dragging. On the route, I don't have time to fiddle with perspective.

**Part 5 is beautiful but dense.**

The "Walk around" buttons are a good idea, but when looking at the Front view in Part 5 (12-part5-example.png), I see 615, 616 on the top floor, then 610-612 below, then 604–607 at ground. That's 8 doors visible and my eye jumps. Real buildings are crowded like this, yeah, but it's exactly why I'd want slight visual separation—a thin shadow or depth marker under each door number. Right now it's flat and hard to parse at a glance. No shadows = no depth = feels like a poster, not a real facade.

**Quiz options don't always feel fair.**

In Part 5, stage 13 (13-part5-quiz-quizopen.png), the options are: 1031, 1431, 1237, 1231. I know the building runs 601–620, so anything in the 1000s is wrong. That's fine for training, but it assumes learners read the intro text carefully. A tighter option set (e.g., 1031, 1032, 1131, 1231) would be more realistic—on the route, I'm not *that* lost. Minor thing.

## The Mobile Question

I didn't see mobile screenshots in the batch, but if this is meant for training in a warehouse or on a driver's phone, the right-side quiz panel would stack down on a 390px screen and block half the building. That's a blocker. Test it.

---

## The Pacing

14 stages is right. Not too short to be trivial, not so long that a rookie checks out. Each part has 2–4 stages and the jump buttons let you skip around. Smart. The Back/Continue/Next flow is clear. Confetti on the final stage is a nice touch—celebratory without being corny.

---

## Real-World Truth

In 18 years, I've found apartments in the dark, rain, on 4G signal, and once in a thunderstorm with a box over my head. Numbering systems vary wildly—some buildings have no logic at all. This course teaches the *five most common* patterns. That's practical. A rookie runs this for 20 minutes, then hits the route with a pattern in their head, and they *will* find doors faster. That's the job.

The course looks good. The progression is solid. The only friction is the text length, the camera grip, the flat visuals, and a mobile test. Fix those three and it's a clean tool.

---

## Summary for the Authors

**Don't move mountains.** The core is working. But:

1. Trim the instruction paragraphs by 30%. Use **bold** for keywords and bullet points for the how.
2. Lock or snap the camera in quiz mode so a learner doesn't accidentally rotate a door number off-screen.
3. Add a faint shadow or depth line under door numbers in dense views (especially Part 5). Makes it feel real.
4. Test on mobile. If the quiz panel stacks below, consider a portrait layout or a narrower panel.

Ship it. Rookies will use it. They'll find apartments faster.
