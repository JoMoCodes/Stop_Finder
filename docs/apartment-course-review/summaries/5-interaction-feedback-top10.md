# Lens: Interaction, Feedback and Reward

The course's core loop — click a blank door, pick an answer, get feedback, move on — is well-liked in principle but under-signaled in practice: reviewers repeatedly said the moment of *finding* something to click, and the moment of *learning why* an answer was wrong, are both weaker than the polish elsewhere would suggest. The reward loop (shake, green glow, confetti) is judged charming but thin — a single celebration at the end of a building rather than momentum along the way. Camera behavior splits the panel: a cinematographer defends the instant "jump cut" camera as intentional and effective, while several first-time and safety-minded voices want more warning before the ground moves under them.

## Discoverability of clickable doors

The single sharpest, most concrete critique in this lens comes from **Dev** (frontend engineer), who flags as his #1, HIGH PRIORITY item that door plates never get a `cursor: pointer` — "users might not realize doors are interactive until they accidentally hover and trigger the quiz. Worse on mobile where there's no hover." **Priya** independently makes the same point in HCI terms: "the 3D scene shows no cursor change, no glow, no outline to signal interactivity," and proposes a faint yellow rim-glow on blank plates plus a fallback "Tip: Click a door with a **?**" hint after inactivity. **Viktor** (game designer) zeroes in on the *partial*-number plates specifically (e.g. "71_5") — right now they "look like a damaged plate, not a puzzle hole" — and suggests a pulsing "?" overlay or a one-time highlight on quiz load. **Kai** wants the same idea applied to the quiz option buttons themselves ("hover effects... make them light up or grow slightly"). Dev also raises a related precision bug: the raycaster targets the *door mesh* as well as the plate, so on dense buildings (Part 5) a click near — but not on — a number opens the quiz unintentionally; he suggests raycasting the plate only.

## Right/wrong feedback — does it explain why?

Consensus is that right-answer feedback is clean (plate updates instantly, green glow) but wrong-answer feedback is emotionally safe and *pedagogically silent*. **Viktor** put it plainly: "wrong answers are forgiving but silent... they don't see *why* it was wrong, or what the correct answer teaches," and proposes surfacing a hint after two wrong tries ("That's the right building, wrong floor"). **Tobias** (learning researcher) traces the same gap to the distractor design itself: options generated by flat numeric offsets often produce choices "so obviously wrong that it teaches nothing," rather than plausible misconceptions (a correct floor but wrong door, say). **Rosa** raised a related, smaller version of this from the driver's-eye view: Part 5's distractors (1031/1431/1237/1231) include implausibly-far-off numbers a real learner wouldn't need help ruling out.

## Timing: the 650ms auto-close

This is one of the few places reviewers actively disagree. **Viktor** calls the 650ms window "crucial — it gives the right answer time to sink in before the panel vanishes," and **Priya** agrees it "strikes a balance between celebration and momentum." **Dev**, however, calls the same hard-coded timeout "user-hostile for accessibility... a learner who reads slowly, or who wants to glance back at their answer, loses it mid-glance," and wants either a manual close (button or ESC) or a longer window. Read together: the timing is well-tuned for a confident learner but has no escape hatch for anyone slower — a fixable gap rather than a wrong choice.

## The shake animation

Three reviewers, three angles on the same 300ms/±5px shake. **Priya** flags it as *subtle* for learners under time pressure or with attention differences, suggesting 400–500ms plus a brief red background flash. **Marcus** (gig driver) worries it may be "too fast or subtle to feel like good feedback" on a phone and wants a red flash or bigger shake. **Kai** is blunter: "0.3 seconds? I barely notice it... wrong answers feel soft." Pulling the other way, **Sam** (accessibility) flags the *opposite* risk: the shake has no `prefers-reduced-motion` guard at all (WCAG 2.3.3), which could be unsafe for vestibular/migraine-sensitive users — his fix is a media query that disables the animation, not a bigger one. Any strengthening of the shake should ship with that motion-preference guard, not instead of it.

## Confetti and completion

Reviewers like the confetti but disagree on whether it's *enough*. **Kai** calls it "the only thing that makes me feel rewarded" and the sole reason the course isn't "boring," while wanting sound and streaks alongside it. **Tobias** is the strongest dissent: confetti "has no connection to the learning goal... a delivery driver does not care about confetti," and proposes replacing or supplementing it with a summary of what was learned ("You solved 14 buildings and learned the five numbering patterns") tied to a real-world payoff. **Viktor** notes the reward is structurally end-loaded: confetti fires once per building, so "if you solve 1 blank out of 3, nothing happens" — he wants a small per-blank reward (particle burst, sound, ticking counter). **Sam** again flags the missing `prefers-reduced-motion` check on the 170-particle burst. **Luna** and **Rosa** are more admiring — Luna wants every small win, not just completion, to feel "celebratory and clear," while Rosa calls the current confetti "celebratory without being corny."

## Per-answer micro-feedback and progress

**Nadia** (cinematographer) makes the most specific ask here: in multi-blank stages (Part 4 has 8 blanks, Part 5 has 9), "there's no persistent visual *mark* on the door itself after solving... the learner can't step back and count: 'I've solved 12 out of 20.'" **Kai** and **Leilani** both want an explicit progress signal — Kai: "I can't see how I'm doing across the whole course... no way to brag"; Leilani proposes a literal progress bar ("Part 1 [2/5 buildings complete]"). **Viktor** suggests a milestone screen after each part ("You've mastered 3-digit units! Next up: building numbers").

## Camera: jump cuts vs. eased transitions, and Back/Next/Start Over

**Nadia** gives the instant-cut camera (`frameStage()`, `setSeqView()`) a strong endorsement as deliberate craft: "no transitions, no easing, no 2-second orbital swirl... it works because it's decisive." She flags only two edge cases: no orbit-damping constraint on example stages (a learner can wild-drag the camera out of frame), and Part 5's default view not matching what the "Front" button shows. Against that, three reviewers want more onboarding *before* the first drag rather than a different camera model: **Eleanor** (first-time user, 74) was startled when the building "whirled around unexpectedly" and wants a line like "Tip: Click and drag... try it now"; **Priya** suggests a brief auto-orbit preview on load; **Luna** suggests a subtle hand-cursor or rotating-arrow hint. **Rosa** goes further and wants an actual behavior change — lock or auto-snap the camera during quiz stages so a dragged-away door number can't cost a rookie the answer. On Back/Next/Start Over: **Priya** and **Omar** both flag the button label switching between "Continue," "Ready," and "Next Building" as confusing rather than meaningful — Omar (ESL) wants one consistent word; Priya suggests either unifying the label or adding a tooltip. **Viktor**, by contrast, reads the label changes as *intentional* progress signaling and lists it under what works.

## Top recommendations for this lens

1. **Add a hover/interaction affordance to clickable doors** — cursor change, glow, or pulse on blank and partial plates (Priya, Dev, Viktor, Kai).
2. **Give wrong answers a "why," not just a shake** — smarter distractors and/or a hint after repeated misses (Viktor, Tobias, Rosa).
3. **Add a persistent progress signal** — solved-door marker, doors-remaining count, or part-level milestone screen (Nadia, Kai, Leilani, Viktor).
4. **Resolve the 650ms auto-close tension** — keep it as a default but add a manual dismiss / longer window for accessibility (Dev, with Viktor/Priya defending the current default).
5. **Strengthen the shake animation, but gate it behind `prefers-reduced-motion`** — don't ship a bigger shake without the motion-safety guard (Priya, Kai, Marcus for strength; Sam for the guard).
6. **Add per-answer or per-building micro-reward beyond one end-of-building confetti burst** — sound, particles, or a ticking count (Kai, Viktor, Luna); respect reduced-motion (Sam).
7. **Onboard the first camera drag** — a one-time hint or short auto-preview rotation before the learner is expected to drag (Eleanor, Priya, Luna); consider Rosa's camera-lock-in-quiz idea as an alternative approach for the same problem.
8. **Fix the raycast precision** so clicks near (not on) a door don't accidentally open the quiz (Dev).
9. **Unify or clarify the Continue/Ready/Next button label** so it reads as one consistent action, especially for non-native readers (Priya, Omar).
10. **Give completion a meaning beyond confetti** — a short "what you learned" summary tied to the real-world task (Tobias), while keeping the confetti visual most reviewers enjoy.

## Out of scope / parked
- Adding real sound effects requires new audio assets — flagged by Kai, Marcus, Eleanor, Luna, but out of scope per the no-build/no-asset-pipeline constraint.
- Haptic/vibration feedback on mobile taps (Kai, Leilani) — needs device APIs beyond this static page's current scope to evaluate.
- A/B testing or formal usability studies with real drivers (Priya, Tobias) — a process recommendation, not a page change.
- Rewriting quiz-distractor *generation logic* to model plausible misconceptions (Tobias) — this is curriculum/algorithm work, not a visual/UX tweak, though it's noted above because it bears directly on feedback quality.

# Cross-cutting Top 10

The ten most-supported visual/UX improvements across all twenty reviews, every topic included, ranked by how many reviewers raised them.

1. **Fix the mobile/responsive layout** — the quiz panel, prompt banner, and 3D scene all break or overlap below ~600px width; several reviewers call it a showstopper. Raised by Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Ingrid, Leilani, Sam, Kai, Bea, Omar, Luna, Nadia, Felix, and Viktor (13 reviewers — the single largest consensus in the set).
2. **Shorten and simplify the prompt-banner instructions** — too many ideas per paragraph, idioms that trip up ESL readers, dense text that wraps badly on small screens. Raised by Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Ingrid, Leilani, Omar, Luna, and Eleanor (8).
3. **Add depth to the flat 3D lighting** — shadows, a sky gradient, and material variation to make the scene feel like a place rather than a diorama on paper. Raised by June, Felix, and Rosa; explicitly defended as an intentional, correct choice by Tobias and Nadia — a genuine split, but a strong ask on one side.
4. **Signal that doors are clickable** — cursor change, hover glow, or pulse on blank/partial plates. Raised by Priya, Dev, Viktor, and Kai (4).
5. **Add a course/building progress indicator** — doors-remaining count, a persistent "solved" mark on filled doors, or a milestone screen between parts. Raised by Kai, Leilani, Viktor, and Nadia (4).
6. **Add sound effects for correct/wrong/completion** — currently the course is entirely silent, which several reviewers say undercuts the "reward" feeling. Raised by Kai, Marcus, Eleanor, and Luna (4).
7. **Onboard the first camera drag** — a hint, cursor cue, or brief preview rotation so new users don't get startled by orbit controls. Raised by Rosa, Eleanor, Priya, and Luna (4).
8. **Fix the numeral typography** — replace the bare `sans-serif` fallback with a real font stack, use tabular figures, and consider digit-place color coding so the numbering pattern is visible in the quiz options themselves. Raised by Ingrid, Ada, and Cyrus (3).
9. **Make wrong-answer feedback stronger and clearer** — a bigger/longer shake or color flash so the "you got it wrong" moment registers. Raised by Priya, Kai, and Marcus (3); balanced by Sam's WCAG requirement that any such animation respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
10. **Unify the Continue/Ready/Next button labeling** so the primary action reads consistently across stages. Raised by Priya and Omar (2), tied with two other twice-raised items worth naming: the "Building #" placeholder that reads like a bug rather than a prompt (Priya, Omar), and quiz distractors that are too easy to eliminate on sight rather than testing real understanding (Rosa, Tobias).
