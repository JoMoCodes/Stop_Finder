# KAI's Apartment Course Review — Delivery Driver Simulator

**Rating: 6.5 / 10** — Good teaching concept, but not enough juice. A solid 5/10 for learning, but only because the confetti saves it from being totally boring.

## What's Actually Cool

The 3D buildings slap. They're simple and colorful, and dragging around to see all the doors feels intuitive. Part 5 especially—spinning around a 20-door building to find the pattern? That's the kind of thing that makes you feel like you're actually solving a puzzle, not just memorizing.

The progressive difficulty is well-designed. Starting with a simple 4-plex (4 doors, easy) and building up to that monster corner building is the right way to teach. The visual hints—the big yellow arrows showing the pattern, the floor badges on Part 2, the "Building 1" banner—are actually helpful and don't feel condescending.

The quiz interface is clean. Four options, you click one, it tells you if you're right. The right answer glows green, the wrong answer goes red. That's clear.

And the confetti at the end of each building? Chef's kiss. Actually nice. Doesn't last forever, but feels good when you nail it.

## What's Weak (aka no juice)

**NO SOUND.** This is the biggest miss. You answer wrong and the button shakes for 0.3 seconds. That's it. No buzz, no error sound, no "wah wah wah" fail horn. It's so quiet that getting something wrong feels like... nothing. No dopamine loss. And when you get it right? Silence. Confetti falls silently. There should be a satisfying "ding" or pop sound. Even something small would help so much.

**No streaks, no score, no progress.** I can't see how I'm doing across the whole course. Are 14 stages a lot? Did I finish one building 100%? I have no idea. There's no "You got 12/14 correct" at the end. No way to brag or feel like you're climbing something. Streaks would make this 100x more addictive—"I got 5 in a row right, gotta keep the streak alive."

**The shake is weak.** 0.3 seconds? I barely notice it. Make it bigger or add a sound and it'll feel like actual punishment for getting it wrong. Right now, wrong answers feel soft.

**Instructions are text walls.** Before each part, there's a big dark banner full of instructions with some words highlighted in amber. It's readable, but not hype. I just want to skip to the building. For a delivery driver learning real fast, that's a lot of reading.

**Mobile layout is rough.** The phone screenshot (Part 1) shows the quiz panel taking up half the screen. On a smaller phone, that might block the building completely. Not cool if someone's trying to learn on a break.

**Building labels can block the view.** "Building 1" appears centered at the top and can hide the roof sometimes, especially on smaller viewports.

## Little Things That Would Help

- Add a **satisfying sound effect** for right/wrong (big one—this alone would bump it to 7.5/10)
- Show a **score/progress bar** during or at the end of each building
- Make the **wrong-answer shake bigger** or add haptic feedback (phone buzz)
- Add **hover effects** to quiz buttons—make them light up or grow slightly when I hover over them
- Show a **streak counter** ("Correct in a row: 3")
- Maybe add **particles or stars** on a right answer, not just confetti at the end
- Smoother **mobile layout**—the quiz panel should slide in from the side or bottom, not take center space

## The Real Talk

This is a solid educational tool. It teaches the apartment-numbering system clearly, and the 3D is way more interesting than flashcards. But it doesn't feel like a game. It feels like a lesson, which is fine—but you said you wanted it to feel like a *game*. Right now, it's missing the feedback loops that make games addictive: sound, visual celebration, score, streaks, and reasons to replay.

The confetti is the only thing that makes me feel rewarded. Everything else is pretty neutral. If I finish a building with one wrong answer vs. all right, I can't tell the difference.

**Biggest thing to fix:** Add sound effects (especially a satisfying "ding" for correct answers and a buzz or pop for confetti). Seriously, that one change could lift this from a 6.5 to a 7.5 overnight. Humans are wired to like sounds—it's half the juice.

## Final Vibe

If this is for training delivery drivers fast, it works. But if you want people to *want* to use it and come back, it needs more juice. Right now, it's a well-made textbook. Make it feel like a game, and it'll stick.
