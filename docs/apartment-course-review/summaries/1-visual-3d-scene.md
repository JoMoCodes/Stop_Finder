# Lens: Visual Design and the 3D Scene

**Executive summary:** Reviewers broadly love the palette, the building silhouettes, and the "walk around" camera navigation, and there's a genuine split — not a simple consensus — on whether the flat, shadowless lighting is a pedagogical strength or a missed opportunity for atmosphere. Two clear, repeated technical complaints cut across many voices: freeform camera drag can leave a building in an unreadable or disorienting position with no way back, and Part 5's side/back faces (and oblique angles generally) are measurably harder to read than the front. The strongest single-voice critique — June Park's color-and-lighting teardown — gives the most concrete, implementable fixes of any review in this batch.

## What reviewers praised

**The palette is the sleeper hit of the whole course.** June Park (colour theorist) calls it "genuinely lovely" — the terracotta siding (`0xb0493a`), sage-olive ground (`0x86996a`), cream roofline band (`0xe7dcc4`), taupe brick (`0xc7b29a`) are "colours that *know* each other." Felix (diorama artist) independently says the palette is "warm and intentional... reads as a cohesive diorama scene, not a computer-graphics default palette." Cyrus (wayfinding), Bea (building super), Luna (poet), Marcus (gig driver), and Rosa (delivery driver) all separately praise the same thing: warm reds/tans, olive ground, restrained amber/yellow UI accents, no jarring or acidic colors. That's seven independent voices — strong consensus.

**Building proportions and typology read as authentic.** Felix says "the proportion and silhouette are bang-on... nailing the *feel* of a type" across the 4-plex, tower, and row. Bea, a real building superintendent, independently confirms this from professional experience: "Part 1 is a duplex/fourplex — seen ten thousand of them. Part 2 is a mid-rise walkup with exterior stairs on the side, exactly like the converted Victorians we manage." Cyrus and Rosa add that the geometry never distorts or clips oddly at any camera angle.

**Camera framing per stage is deliberate, not arbitrary.** Nadia (cinematographer) gives the deepest read: the 50°-FOV "normal lens" avoids distortion, and each stage's starting camera position is "motivated" — low/upward for the small 4-plex to "give small buildings authority," a side angle for the tower "so the 4-story climb is readable as a vertical stack." Hiro (geometer) and Viktor (game designer) independently confirm the per-stage camera placements are orthogonal to each building type and "pedagogically sound," with no unwanted foreshortening.

**The Part 5 "Walk Around" (Front/Back/Left/Right) camera-jump buttons are the single most-praised interaction in the whole batch.** Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Bea, Cyrus, Hiro, Nadia, and Luna all call it out — 8 reviewers, easily the strongest consensus finding in this review. Nadia frames the instant, un-eased cuts between views as intentional "news-editor cutting... decisive," while Bea says it "solves a category of confusion I see constantly" because real buildings don't just have a front.

## Where opinions split: flat lighting

This is the most substantive disagreement in the batch. **Critics** — June, Felix, and Rosa — argue the flat, shadowless lighting (hemisphere light at 0.9, ambient at 0.25, a sun that "barely shadows") makes the scene "feel like a carefully flattened diorama" (Felix) or "a stage backdrop that's been lit to death" (June). Rosa notes concretely that in dense Part 5 views, doors have "no shadows = no depth = feels like a poster, not a real facade," and asks for "a faint shadow or depth line under door numbers." June gives the most technical prescription of any review: enable `castShadow`/`receiveShadow`, drop the hemisphere light to 0.6, warm the top light toward `0xfff5e6`, add a sky gradient, vary roughness (brick 0.88, doors/windows 0.95), and cool the road strip toward `0x7a8a9a` so it recedes.

**Defenders** — Priya, Tobias, Viktor, and Nadia — argue flatness is a deliberate, correct choice. Priya: "a learner won't miss a blank because it's in shadow... for a teaching tool, readability > realism." Tobias: "This is *not* photorealistic, but it is precisely the right choice for learning... the flat lighting ensures every door plate is equally readable from every angle." Nadia goes furthest, calling it "teaching light, not beauty light" and praising the minimal, textureless ground plane as intentional "negative space" that keeps the eye on the building rather than competing detail. **Synthesis:** the disagreement is really about audience and priority — everyone agrees flat lighting maximizes plate legibility; the question is whether legibility and atmosphere have to trade off, or whether shadows/gradient sky could be added without hurting readability (as June and Felix both explicitly argue).

## Other recurring or single-voice findings

- **Doors and windows lack depth cues.** Rosa ("the green door plate is nearly as dark as the black windows; there's no shadow cavity to tell me the door sits back"), June ("door recesses don't read as recesses"), and Felix ("windows... are a bit shy [of catching light]") independently flag the same flatness symptom on doors/windows specifically — three voices, consensus.
- **Ground and geometry read as too clean/abstract.** June calls the ground "a paint chip"; Felix says it "feels like a game board, not a street" and wants curbs/weathering; Bea separately worries the *whole* building is "too tidy" — every door perfectly aligned, no chipped paint, no missing digits — which could leave a driver unprepared for messier real buildings. Three voices converge on "too pristine," though for different underlying reasons (atmosphere vs. training realism).
- **Freeform camera drag can strand the learner in a bad view, with no reset.** Rosa asks to "lock the front face in quiz mode, or snap back... after 2 seconds of no dragging" so a rotated door number isn't missed. Eleanor (first-time user) describes the building "whirling around unexpectedly" and wanting a guided first-drag hint. Nadia flags the identical technical gap from the code side: "no orbit damping" safeguard on example stages means a learner can drag the building fully out of frame with no reset-view button. Viktor separately recommends verifying damping/inertia feels good on touch. Four independent voices on the same underlying gap.
- **Part 5 and oblique angles are measurably harder to read.** Hiro's geometric analysis shows the 20-door layout is bilaterally asymmetric (left wall reads front-to-back, right wall reads back-to-front), which "breaks intuition" even with orthogonal camera views; he suggests a top-down schematic diagram. Cyrus independently flags that side-facing canvas text "pixelates under perspective" and proposes pre-skewed textures or higher resolution for oblique plates. Bea and Rosa both separately note numbers get cramped/hard-to-parse in Part 5's dense views. Four voices.
- **Staircases feel like an afterthought.** Bea, drawing on 30 years managing buildings, wants the stairs to be a "chunky," visually prominent real-world landmark rather than "painted on." Felix separately agrees: staircase geometry is "functional but not eye-catching," calling it a place model-makers usually "flex." Two strong voices.
- **The floating "Building #" banner reads as disconnected/broken.** Bea says it looks like "a UFO" hovering over the roof rather than signage painted on a real facade; Priya independently notes the gray-on-gray "#" placeholder "looks like a typo... rather than a prompt." Two voices, overlaps with the typography lens.
- **No persistent "solved" indicator on doors** in multi-blank stages — once a plate fills in, there's no glow/checkmark to show at-a-glance progress across a dense building. Single strong voice: Nadia.
- **Sky lacks atmospheric depth** — a single detailed voice, June: "washed-out periwinkle... not light blue like a real sky... no sense of air or distance." Worth weighing alongside the lighting debate above rather than as a separate fix.

## Top recommendations for this lens

1. **Resolve the lighting debate with a middle path: keep plates fully lit/legible, but add directional shadow and a touch of contact-shadow under buildings and door recesses.** (June, Felix, Rosa — critics; Priya/Tobias/Nadia's legibility concern can be preserved by shadowing the environment, not the plates.)
2. **Add a camera safety net: auto-recenter after a few seconds of no drag, or a "reset view" affordance, especially in quiz stages.** (Rosa, Eleanor, Nadia, Viktor)
3. **Address Part 5's asymmetric/oblique readability**, e.g. a schematic diagram of the 20-unit loop, and/or higher-resolution or pre-skewed textures for side-facing plates. (Hiro, Cyrus, Bea, Rosa)
4. **Give doors and windows real depth cues** (shadowed recesses, brighter/bigger window glazing) without touching plate contrast. (Rosa, June, Felix)
5. **Add subtle ground texture/variation** (asphalt detail, slight weathering, a cooler road strip) while preserving the minimal "negative space" Nadia praises. (June, Felix; weigh against Nadia's defense of the plain ground)
6. **Make the building-name banner feel anchored to the architecture** rather than a floating placeholder — mount it to the facade, and fix the "#" placeholder text. (Bea, Priya)
7. **Give staircases more visual weight/detail as a real-world landmark.** (Bea, Felix)
8. **Keep and, if possible, extend the Walk Around camera-jump pattern** — it's the most consensus-praised element in this whole review batch and a template for handling Part 4's freeform "spin to see the back" too. (Rosa, Marcus, Priya, Bea, Cyrus, Hiro, Nadia, Luna)
9. **Add a lightweight per-door "solved" visual state** (glow/checkmark) for dense multi-blank stages so progress is visible at a glance. (Nadia)
