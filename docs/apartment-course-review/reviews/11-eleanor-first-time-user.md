# A Courier's First Look: Eleanor Whitcombe's Review of the Apartment Course

Dear, I have to say — I'm quite impressed, even if I got a bit lost along the way. I've never done anything like this before, these 3D things weren't part of my life, and I'll be honest: some parts made me flustered. But I can see how useful this would be for someone like me learning the ropes on the job.

## What's Working Well

The **yellow arrows in Part 1** are absolutely splendid — they show you *how* to read the building, which is exactly what I needed. That's the kind of thing that made me think, "Oh, *I* understand now." Same with the **floor badges on Part 2** — little numbered badges (1, 2, 3, 4) down the side tell you which floor is which. Very clever. I could see that, and it made the pattern click.

The **top banner with the instructions** — dark background with bright amber text — is easy to read. I can see the keywords stand out (they're bold and golden), so even at my age, when my eyes aren't what they were, I knew what to look for. The **part buttons at the top left** (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) are large enough, and they light up green to show me where I am. That's reassuring.

The **quiz panel on the right side** — when you click a door marked "?", up pops the four choices (a, b, c, d). The options are big, readable, and when you pick wrong, something shakes or shows red. Feedback, that's what you call it. I always knew what happened. And the **building number labels** at the top of each screen ("Building 1," "Building 6," etc.) — those are huge and clear.

## Where I Got Stuck

Here's the thing: **the 3D buildings themselves**. Now, I can *see* them, don't get me wrong. They're quite nice, honestly — pleasant colors, simple shapes. But to me, they look like I'm looking at the building from a strange angle, like I'm floating up in the air. In Part 1, there's just one small building, so it's fine. But by Part 3 and Part 4, when the buildings get bigger and I have to turn the camera around... well, that's where I got flustered.

The instructions say "Drag to look around." I tried clicking and dragging, and sometimes the building *whirled* around unexpectedly. I think I know what happened — my mouse dragged farther than I thought — but it startled me. The screen just *changes*, you know? And nobody told me why. If it had said, "You can drag the mouse side-to-side to spin the building around — try it now!" I would have felt braver about it. Instead, I felt like I was doing something wrong.

**The mobile screenshot** (the narrow phone one) was particularly hard to read. The building became so small, and the prompt text at the top was cut off and running together. I wouldn't want to take a *real* course on a phone. Maybe add a note that says "Best on a tablet or computer"?

## Smaller Things

- **The quiz options** appear so quickly when I click a door that I almost didn't see them pop up at first. A tiny animation or sound might help — nothing loud, just something to say, "Hey, look here now."

- **Part 5's "Walk around" buttons** (Front, Back, Left, Right) — those are helpful for someone who can't easily drag and spin. But the buttons are only *on* Part 5. I'm wondering if earlier parts could use something like that, too. Or at least a hint: "Can't see the back? Try holding and dragging, or [keyboard shortcut]." I got by, but I felt unsure.

- **The unit numbers on the door plates** — I could read them most of the time, but on the bigger buildings (Part 3 and beyond), some of the numbers seemed a bit small. Maybe a tiny bit larger would help folks with glasses like me.

- **The prompt banner text is quite dense.** In Part 3, for instance, there's a lot of information: building numbers, floor numbers, reading order. I had to read it twice. A little more line-spacing, or breaking it into two sentences, might help. I'm not slow, I just don't see as well, and my brain needs a second to parse it all.

## What Gives Me Confidence

The **green buttons at the bottom** ("Continue," "Ready," "Back") are reassuring. They're clearly the *action* buttons, and they work as expected. When I finished all the quizzes, I trusted that clicking "Next Building" would move me on — and it did. That consistency is wonderful.

The **building shells and colors** are plain and straightforward — no fancy tricks or shadows that might confuse my eyes. A tan building, a red building, green doors. Simple. Honest. I can focus on the *numbers*, which is the point.

And I loved that **the course is split into five parts.** Each one teaches something new, building on what came before. I never felt thrown in the deep end (though Part 4 came close!). If someone like me can work through Parts 1, 2, and 3, I know I can handle a real apartment building when I'm out making deliveries.

## If I Could Change One Thing

**Please add a moment of guidance before I have to drag the camera for the first time.** Maybe a little on-screen hint: *"Tip: Click and drag on the building to spin it around. Try it now."* Or even better, a `<kbd>?</kbd>` button that explains all the controls at once — drag, zoom, button navigation — so I'm not guessing. The younger folks might figure it out instinctively, but someone like me needs to know it's *okay* to try, and *how*.

## In Summary

This is a **solid, well-thought-out teaching tool.** The visuals are clear, the progression is sensible, and the feedback is honest. My eyes are 74 years old and not as sharp as they used to be, and I got through it with only minor fumbles — that's a good sign. The buildings are charming, the instructions are kind, and the whole thing feels like someone who *cares* made it.

I'm ready to go out and count some apartment numbers.

— Eleanor
